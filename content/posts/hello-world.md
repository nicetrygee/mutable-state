Hey! I'm Gregg. I've been working in software engineering for 26 years. In recent years, I've rarely written anything longer than a design doc.

That changed recently, at a big life inflection point when I left my perfectly good job as an Engineering Manager at a Data Engineering consultancy in London and decided to embark on an adventurous life change by moving to Sydney. I never had a job to go to, and just in time for one of the worst hiring markets anyone can remember in Sydney. Layoffs everywhere, hundreds of applicants per role, and recruiters ghosting me. Ask me to pick a worse time to move to the other side of the planet and I'd struggle.

So I started writing. Partly because it keeps my brain busy, and partly because I have opinions and nowhere to put them. I've never been a person who works out what they think by talking it through in a meeting. I work it out by writing it down, trashing half of it and trying again until I'm happy with it. My thinking always happens through writing. I've just never published any of it to the big bad web for fear of being ridiculed. But my industry is in so much flux at the moment that I need to write (and building) to try and make sense of it, and also where I want to be in the next chapter of my career. More importantly, I need to share what I learn and what I think with the people who are going through the same things as me so maybe we can help each other.

I find writing is hard on beliefs. An idea that sounds perfectly sensible in my head often falls apart once I commit to text. Gaps rear their heads and it becomes a game of whack-a-mole. The claim I've repeated in my head for weeks with no evidence behind it. The point that's technically correct but actually boring as all hell. All of this results in binning off more drafts than I publish, and deservedly so my friends! Writing changes what I believe at least as much as my beliefs shape the writing. That's an awkward thing to admit when I'm paid to have opinions.

My process isn't sophisticated. I pull on whatever thread I can't stop thinking about and I follow it until I either have something to say or realise I don't. I try to publish regularly, and I don't try to guess what will land or chase what's trending. I write for me. I write to understand, and to share that understanding with others.
