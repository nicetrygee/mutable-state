I'm Gregg. I've been working in software engineering for 26 years. For most of that time I've rarely written anything longer than a design doc.

That changed recently, at a big life inflection point when I left my perfectly good job as an Engineering Manager and Delivery Manager at a Data Engineering consultancy in London and decided to embark on an adventurous life change by moving to Sydney. I don't have a job to go to, just in time for one of the worst hiring markets anyone can remember. Layoffs everywhere, hundreds of applicants for every single role, and recruiters ghosting me. Ask me to pick a worse time to move to the other side of the planet and I'd struggle.

So I've started writing. Partly because it keeps my brain busy, and partly because I have opinions and nowhere to put them. I've never been a person who works out what they think by talking it through in a meeting. I work it out by writing it down, trashing half of it out and trying again. My thinking always happens through writing. I've just never published any of it outside of the company I've been working with at the time.

Writing is hard on beliefs. An idea that sounds perfectly sensible in my head often falls apart once i commit it to text and it becomes a paragraph with a beginning, a middle and an end. The gaps rear their pesky heads and it becomes a game of whack-a-mole. The claim I've repeated in my head for weeks with no evidence behind it. The argument that only works if nobody looks too closely. The point that's technically correct but actually boring as all hell. All of this results in binning off more drafts than I publish, and deservedly so my friends! Writing changes what I believe at least as much as my beliefs shape the writing. That's an awkward thing to admit when I've been paid to have opinions for a long time. 

My process isn't sophisticated. I pull on whatever thread I can't stop thinking about. It's usually somewhere where engineering, AI and how teams work overlap. I follow it until I either have something to say or realise I don't. I try to publish regularly, but I don't try to guess what will land or chase what's trending. I write for me, on the assumption that if something keeps me up at night, maybe it'll interest someone else too.
