---
title: Engineering Management in the Age of Continuous Judgement
date: 2026-01-22
---  


AI isn't just making engineers more productive. It's changing where and how often judgement happens.

When implementation becomes significantly cheaper, software engineering stops being a sequence of decisions followed by execution. It becomes a continuous stream of judgement where every prompt is a design decision. Every generated diff is a trade-off, and every accepted change is a validation of your understanding of the system.

That has an important consequence. Engineering Management is no longer operating in a world where judgement is occasional and buffered by implementation. Judgement is now continuous, embedded and high-frequency. The Role doesn't fundamentally change but the density of what it has always been responsible for increases.


### Engineering management was never about ownership of judgement
Some have held a belief that Engineering Managers make better decisions than engineers but that was never true. The role has always been about improving the conditions in which good judgement emerges:

 - improving how judgement is formed.
 - increasing consistency of decision-making.
 - reducing variance across teams.
 - creating systems where good decisions become more likely.


### The collapse of the implementation buffer

Software engineering once had a natural buffer between judgement and consequence:

Design → Build → Discover → Iterate

Implementation absorbed uncertainty and created time for reflection but AI has compressed that buffer. Implementation is nearly instant, alternatives are cheaper to explore, and iteration happens continuously. The pause between deciding and seeing the result is now far shorter. So, Engineering has shifted from staged judgement to continuous judgement.


### What changes in practice
Engineering Management shifts from coordinating work to calibrating systems.

Three capabilities become increasingly important:

Framing. Poorly framed problems produce incorrect answers faster. Good framing constrains exploration.

Validation. Cheap output makes accepting incorrect output more expensive. The question becomes not can we generate code, but how do we know it's good enough?

Coherence. AI accelerates individuals, but also increases divergence in mental models. Maintaining shared understanding becomes more valuable.

These responsibilities aren't new. They're simply exercised more often and with greater consequence.


### What gets compressed
Not everything scales.

  - task breakdown becomes increasingly automated.
  - status reporting loses value when work is continuously visible.
  - micro-coordination is absorbed into tooling.
  - delivery metrics matter less than system health and quality signals.

In my opinion, execution management shrinks and judgement management grows.


### The implication for EM's
The cost of poor judgement is rising faster than the cost of execution.Engineers exercise judgement more frequently. AI generates plausible but incorrect solutions at scale. Systems evolve faster than humans can manually verify them.

In that environment, the value of Engineering Management is no longer directing work. It's stabilising the quality of judgement under increasing speed. Their influence shifts from making decisions to shaping the conditions in which good decisions happen repeatedly.
